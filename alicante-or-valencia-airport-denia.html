Changeover day: what a proper turnaround between guests involves

DPC
Denia Property Care
26 June 2026 · 7 min read

Between one guest leaving and the next arriving there is often a four-hour window. Inside that window sits the entire impression your next guest will take away, and there is very little room for error. A reading of the property at 1pm gives you a different story than a reading at 6pm, and that's why the first check matters so much.

The sequence

  • Guest out — keys returned and the property checked while the car is still in the drive.
  • Strip and reset linen and towels, counted in and counted out.
  • Clean, with bathrooms and kitchen done last so nothing is walked back through.
  • Inspection: damage, missing items, anything the previous guests moved or broke.
  • Restock consumables and welcome items.
  • Set the property for arrival — air conditioning, shutters, pool lights, terrace.
  • Guest in, with a handover that answers questions before they're asked.

The step owners skip

It's the check at the very start, while the previous guests are still loading the car. Damage found then is a conversation; damage found after the next guests arrive is a dispute, and often the dispute is between you and the guest rather than between the guest and the previous guests.

Why same-day matters

A cleaner who arrives "sometime on Saturday" and a pool service booked "for the Saturday morning" are two jobs sharing one small window, a washing machine, a hot water tank and a road. Sequence matters. The property has to be left in a state where the next arrival can walk through it without needing a rescue plan.

Reviews aren't written during the stay. They're written in the ten minutes after the guest opens the front door.

Changeovers & guest support

Cleaning coordination, key handover, final inspection and a local contact for your guests for the whole stay.
