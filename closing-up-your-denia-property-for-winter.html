Closing up your Denia property for winter: the full checklist

DPC
Denia Property Care
15 September 2026 · 8 min read

Closing a Spanish property for three to four months is routine if you follow the steps in order. Missing one—especially plumbing or gas—can turn an empty house into a repair bill that runs into thousands. This checklist covers what to do, when, and why it matters.

Water system: drain, don't just switch off

Pipes freeze in continental winters, and frozen pipes burst. Draining the water entirely is the only way to avoid it. Close the main stopcock, open every tap in the house (including external ones and the kitchen sprayer), and leave them open. The house will dry inside as water settles. If your property has a pool, speak to a contractor about winterisation—chlorine levels and pump maintenance are specific and worth doing right.

Heating and boiler: switch to minimal mode

A modern combi boiler set to 10°C frost protection will fire up enough to keep pipes from freezing without heating the house. Older systems may not have that option. If yours doesn't, have a technician set it before you leave. Turning a boiler off entirely is a false economy; the repair cost for a frozen system is always higher than the gas cost for frost protection.

Electricity: leave the essentials on

Kill the main breaker for the stove and any high-draw appliances, but leave the circuit for the boiler and alarm (if you have one) live. A dark, electrically dead house is an invitation. A house with an active alarm that can call you is a deterrent. Switching everything off to save money on winter electricity is a common mistake that ends up costing far more.

Gas: check everything twice

Gas is not something to guess about. If you have a gas boiler, it's already checked. If you have gas for cooking or hot water backup, hire a technician to isolate the gas supply at the meter. Don't try to identify and close individual appliance valves—you'll waste time and risk doing it wrong. A two-minute job at €40 is always cheaper than the aftermath.

Windows and doors: inspection before closing

Walk the perimeter. Check every window frame and door seal for cracks or gaps that might let water in during a winter storm. Denia doesn't get serious flooding, but wind-driven rain into a gap can cause mold months later. If you spot anything, have a joiner look at it before you leave. It's much easier to fix when the house is empty than when you're trying to arrange it from abroad.

Exterior: gutters and drainage

Clear gutters of leaves and debris. Check that downpipes are clear and discharging water away from the house base. A blocked gutter that overflows will run down the exterior wall into the foundations over months—you won't see the damage until spring when it's expensive to put right.

Appliances: empty and prop open

Empty the fridge and leave it unplugged with the door ajar. Empty the freezer the same way. Remove anything from washing machine and dishwasher drums. Damp, sealed cavities breed mold. An open door with light inside stops mold and pest nesting.

A small repair done in October is cheaper than a large one discovered in April.

A final walk-through

  • Water main closed and taps open.
  • Boiler set to frost protection.
  • Alarm set and circuit live.
  • Gas isolated at meter.
  • Gutters clear, downpipes running.
  • Windows and doors inspected and sealed.
  • All appliances empty, doors open or off.
  • Lights on a timer (optional but useful).
  • Insurance confirmed for winter vacancy.
  • Emergency contact left with a trusted neighbour.
