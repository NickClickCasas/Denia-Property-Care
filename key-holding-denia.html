Why a pool looks tired by August — and what a weekly visit catches first

DPC
Denia Property Care
30 July 2026 · 6 min read

By the middle of August we're usually being called to pools that, on paper, were being looked after. The pattern is almost always the same: nothing failed suddenly. Several small things drifted out of tune and the pool quietly became harder work. In a property that is staring at a long summer of use, that small decline is exactly what costs owners money.

August is the hardest month for a pool

Peak heat, peak bather load, wind-blown dust off the terraces, and — in a rental — a changeover every Saturday. Equipment that coasts through May gets no rest in August. The combination of heat, use, and poor maintenance rhythm is what produces tired-looking water, compromised chemistry, and pump strain.

What a weekly visit is really checking

  • Water level, because a pool that drops below the skimmer pulls air and the pump dislikes it.
  • The filter and pump running pressures and hours — a number that moves is telling you something.
  • Skimmers, baskets and drains, cleared before they become a blockage.
  • Water clarity and balance, tested rather than eyeballed.
  • The pump's sound and the plant room for leaks, which is where a cheap fix becomes an expensive one.
  • Surrounds, grilles, ladders and fencing — the parts guests touch.

The signs owners spot too late

If you arrive once a month, you see a pool that either looks fine or looks broken, with very little in between. The useful information lives in that in-between space: slightly hazy water, a knot of debris at the skimmer, a filter that has been running too long, a pump that sounds laboured. Weekly visits catch this before it becomes a visible decline.

If you rent the property out

Pool condition is one of the few things a guest will photograph and put in a review. The pool is also the first thing that suffers when a changeover runs late. A service visit on the same day as the cleaner and linen refresh is not a luxury: it is how you stop a good-looking property from becoming an average one.

Pools rarely break. They complain for a while first, and someone has to be there to hear it.
