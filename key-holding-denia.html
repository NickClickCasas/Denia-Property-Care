Key holding is not a spare key in a drawer

DPC
Denia Property Care
6 August 2026 · 8 min read

A friend of an owner's friend, holding a key in a desk drawer three countries away, is not key holding. It's hopeful thinking. Real key holding is infrastructure: secure storage, a log, a chain of custody, and someone who can be on site when you can't.

What professional key holding actually involves

  • Secure storage. Keys kept in a locked cabinet, not a drawer, with restricted access and insurance backing it.
  • A log. Every time a key leaves the building, it's recorded: who, what for, when returned. This protects you both ways — you have evidence of responsible handling, and a record of who was in your property.
  • Guest coordination. A renter arrives at the airport; they text the service. The key is ready to hand over on arrival, or waiting in a secure lockbox if timing doesn't align. No guest standing in the heat waiting for someone to find their phone.
  • Contractor access. The plumber needs in on Tuesday morning. You don't have to be awake. Someone checks the property is secure before and after.
  • Real estate agent access. A Click Casas agent needs to show the property mid-week to a buyer. Keys are retrieved and returned, with a log entry. You're not trying to coordinate across time zones.
  • Insurance alignment. Most insurers want to know you're using a service, not leaving keys "somewhere safe." A professional service satisfies that requirement and can prove it.

Why the log matters

Imagine something goes missing, or there's unexplained wear. Without a log, you're in a conversation with your insurer where nobody can say who was actually in the property. With a log, you can say: "Keys left the building three times that month, on these dates, for these reasons." It's the difference between a claim that might not pay and one that clearly does.

For rental properties especially

Guests often check in late or early. A family driving from a ferry wants to drop bags at 2pm, not wait until 5pm for someone to be free. A key service that handles 3pm check-ins as easily as 5pm ones is doing logistics work you can't do from abroad — and a guest with a smooth check-in is a guest who leaves a good review.

The thing owners often miss

Professional key holding isn't about the keys. It's about not having to worry. You're not checking your email at midnight wondering if the plumber got in. You're not trying to arrange a video call to talk someone through finding a hidden key box. You're not explaining to your insurer why a key was lying around when something went wrong.

Key holding is the thing you notice by its absence. The first time a guest can't get in, or you can't reach anyone, you understand why it matters.

Who uses key holding

Owners who rent the property out, owners who are often abroad, owners who have contractors working (plumber, electrician, gardener, pool man), and owners whose properties are managed by a real estate agent. So: most owners.

What it typically costs

A professional key holding service usually runs €15–30 per month, sometimes with a small charge per access (€3–5). Set against the cost of a guest cancelling because they couldn't get in, or an emergency repair that didn't happen because nobody could let the emergency plumber in, it's not expensive insurance.
