The Spanish tax calendar for non-resident owners, in plain English

DPC
Denia Property Care
14 August 2026 · 10 min read

The single most common misconception we hear from owners is that paying IBI settles their Spanish tax obligations for the year. It doesn't. IBI is a local tax paid to the town hall; the Modelo 210, wealth-tax reporting, and any rental declarations are separate obligations. We see the confusion repeatedly because the IBI bill is the one that arrives in the post box and feels like the final word. It isn't.

IBI — the local property tax

  • Rate: 0.4%–1.1% of the cadastral value (valor catastral), with the rate set by each town hall.
  • Billed annually by the Ayuntamiento, usually with a payment window in the autumn.
  • Your cadastral value appears on the IBI receipt — keep it, because you need it for your Modelo 210.

If you don't have the receipt to hand, or you're not sure the cadastral value is current, that's worth chasing: both IBI and imputed income are calculated from that figure.

Modelo 210 — the one owners forget

If your Spanish property is empty or used only by you and your family, Spain still assumes it generates a notional "imputed" income, and taxes you on it. An unrented holiday home does not avoid the tax just because nobody has stayed there.

  • Taxable base: 1.1% of cadastral value if it has been revised in the last 10 years — otherwise 2%.
  • Rate: 19% for residents of the EU, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein; 24% for other non-residents, including the UK.
  • Deadline: by 31 December of the following year, filed on Modelo 210.
  • The tax authority does not send you a bill. It is self-assessed.

If you rent it out for part of the year

The regime changes rather than the numbers. Rental income is declared on the same form but under different rules, and EU/EEA owners can generally deduct allowable expenses such as IBI, community fees, and maintenance costs. If you let the property for part of the year and use it yourself for the rest, you file a rental return for the let days and an imputed-income return for the vacant days. Missing that second piece is where the trouble starts.

Wealth tax

Wealth tax applies once your net Spanish assets exceed €700,000 in the relevant thresholds, and is filed separately. For most owners of a single apartment or villa it doesn't bite — but it is not a tax you can ignore if your overall Spanish asset base is large enough. It's another reason to keep the paperwork in one place.

Why we mention taxes on a property care blog

Because half of the practical side is paperwork you can't do from another country. Someone has to hold the IBI receipt, notice the official letter that looks like junk mail, let the gestor know what is due, and keep the property in order while the tax calendar ticks through the year.

None of these dates move to accommodate you being overseas.
